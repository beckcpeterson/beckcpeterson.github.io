01 / PORT OF LOS ANGELES

Financial benchmarking · Debt and treasury

Port Financial Strength & ACTA Obligations

I compared the financial and operating positions of the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach to evaluate their capacity to meet shared Alameda Corridor obligations.

Six fiscal years analyzed · Debt profiles projected through 2044 and 2054 · Executive presentation delivered

View case study
The question

How did the two ports compare financially and operationally, and which was better positioned to absorb potential shortfall payments tied to the Alameda Corridor Transportation Authority?

My contribution
  • Updated three income-statement models, ratio analysis, minimum annual guarantees, and debt-service summaries.
  • Reviewed senior bond official statements and created cash-flow forecasts and capital-improvement projections.
  • Connected port operations, liquidity, capital spending, debt schedules, and ACTA obligations in one comparative analysis.
Analysis & finding

I compared cargo volumes, revenue growth, operating margins, debt-service coverage, days cash on hand, capital plans, and future debt requirements. The analysis indicated that Los Angeles was better positioned to absorb shared ACTA obligations because of its stronger coverage, liquidity, and shorter debt schedule.

Deliverable & value

I converted the work into a 16-slide executive presentation and presented it to leaders across Finance, Accounting, and Debt & Treasury, including the CFO and other senior personnel.

01 / ECONOMETRICS

Applied econometrics · Housing markets

Housing Prices, Metro Access & Neighborhood Amenities

I used multivariate regression to estimate how property age, transit access, and nearby convenience stores were associated with housing prices in New Taipei City.

414 observations · R² of 59.5% · Explicit treatment of model limitations

View case study
The question

How were unit-area housing prices related to house age, distance from the nearest metro station, and the number of nearby convenience stores?

My approach
  • Analyzed 414 housing transactions from 2012 and 2013 in R.
  • Log-transformed metro distance to improve linearity.
  • Added a quadratic age term after identifying a nonlinear relationship.
  • Tested and rejected a more complex categorical treatment of convenience stores when it did not improve fit.
Findings

The final OLS model explained approximately 59.5% of price variation. One additional nearby store was associated with an approximately 0.77-unit increase in price, while a 1% increase in metro distance was associated with an approximately 0.067-unit decrease.

Interpretation

I treated the findings as associations rather than causal effects and discussed omitted variables including property size, renovation status, building quality, school quality, safety, and exact location.

02 / INTERNATIONAL FINANCE

Monetary policy · Digital assets

Stablecoins, Monetary Policy & the Future of Finance

I examined how stablecoins could affect payments, banking, Treasury markets, short-term interest rates, regulation, and the international reach of the dollar.

29-slide research presentation · Global policy and market perspective

View case study
The question

Could stablecoins become a mainstream financial tool, and how might their growth affect banks, money markets, monetary policy, regulation, and international finance?

My research
  • Compared fiat-backed, commodity-backed, crypto-collateralized, and algorithmic models.
  • Evaluated payment efficiency, financial access, reserve quality, de-pegging, security, and contagion risks.
  • Compared adoption and regulatory approaches across the United States, Europe, China, and the UAE.
Economic mechanism

I traced how inflows can cause issuers to purchase Treasury bills and how large redemptions can force asset sales. Those transactions can influence short-term yields, liquidity, and funding costs, particularly when redemptions are concentrated among a small number of large participants.

Conclusion

I concluded that properly regulated, fully backed stablecoins are more likely to complement traditional banks than replace them. They may improve payment efficiency and extend dollar usage, while reserve transparency, liquidity, redemption rights, and concentration remain key policy concerns.
